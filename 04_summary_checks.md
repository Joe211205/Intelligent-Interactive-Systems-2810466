# Summary checks

For two verified papers: the assistant's three-sentence summary of the abstract, and your mark on each sentence.

Prompt:

```
Here is the abstract of a paper I have verified: <paste abstract>.

Summarise it in three sentences: what they did, what they found, what it means for <my problem>. Do not add anything that is not in the abstract.
```

Marks: SUPPORTED (write the abstract phrase beside it) · NOT IN ABSTRACT (strike it, or keep it marked as your own inference) · CONTRADICTED (the abstract says otherwise: strike it and log it).

## Paper 1: <Janssen, M. et al. (2020) ‘Will algorithms blind people? the effect of explainable AI and decision-makers’ experience on AI-supported decision-making in government’, Social Science Computer Review, 40(2), pp. 478–493. doi:10.1177/0894439320980118.>
| Sentence | Mark | Abstract phrase, or note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The researchers experimentally compared human decision-making without algorithmic support, with business-rule support, and with machine-learning support, allowing both algorithms to provide correct or incorrect recommendations. | SUPPORTED | 	“compare decision making in three situations… without any support… supported by business rules… supported by machine learning. BR and ML algorithms could provide correct or incorrect suggestions to the decision maker.”
| 2 | They found that algorithms improved decision accuracy and that explainable AI combined with experience helped participants identify incorrect recommendations, although even experienced participants failed to detect all mistakes. | SUPPORTED | “algorithms help decision makers to make more correct decisions… explainable AI combined with experience helps them detect incorrect suggestions… even experienced persons were not able to identify all mistakes.”
| 3 | For the question of whether increasing AI explainability or transparency helps people find mistakes, the findings suggest that it can help, particularly when combined with experience, but it does not eliminate errors, meaning that careful algorithm selection and decision-maker training remain important. | SUPPORTED | “explainable AI combined with experience helps them detect incorrect suggestions… even experienced persons were not able to identify all mistakes… algorithms should be adopted with care… selecting the appropriate algorithms… and training of decision makers are key factors.”

## Paper 2: <Xu, M. and Wang, Y. (2024) ‘Explainability increases trust resilience in Intelligent Agents’, British Journal of Psychology, 117(2), pp. 528–547. doi:10.1111/bjop.12740.>
