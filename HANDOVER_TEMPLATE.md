# HANDOVER.md — template

Copy to `HANDOVER.md` and fill in. Written for the person — or the run — that picks this up next,
who was not here and cannot ask you anything.

Six fields, from Lecture 15. A handover is a place where information is lost, and the loss is
invisible on both sides: the one handing over believes it passed everything on, and the one
receiving has no way to know what it was not told. These six are what usually goes missing.

---

```markdown
# Handover — [task, in five words]

**Task.** [One sentence, imperative. The completion condition, as the command.]

**Resolved, and by whom.** [What is now decided that was open before, and who decided it — you, the
agent, or a test. "The fix reads the source field rather than editing corpus/ — decided by me,
because corpus/ is read-only." This is the field people leave out, and it is the one that matters.]

**Constraints.** [The tool boundary, and anything the next person must not undo.]

**Done.** [What is actually true now. Quote the command output, not your impression of it.]

**Next.** [The one thing to do next, small enough to start.]

**Budget left.** [Steps, time, or approvals remaining. If you do not track one, say so — "no budget
set; the run was stopped by hand after 11 steps" is an honest answer and a useful one.]
```

---

Two checks before you call it done:

- Could someone who was not in the room carry on from this alone? If not, which field is thin?
- Does it contain anything private — a real name, an email address, a full path with your username?
  Take it out now, not later.
